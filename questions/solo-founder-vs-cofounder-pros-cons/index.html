What are the pros and cons of being a solo founder?

"If you're a person like me, I do think solo founder life is the best life really" — but Rakhesh is blunt about the cost: day to day can be "really hard, really lonely," and "you are the emotional regulator of your company." Rakhesh actually had a co-founder originally, and they had to part ways because the fit on work ethic and standards wasn't there. His bar now: unless there was somebody who understood what they're shooting for, cared about the quality, and could hustle as hard as he'd want them to, solo is better. The hardest part is emotional: "if you've just taken a massive punch to the face and then you go into a meeting to talk to your team and that bruise is showing, the whole team feels it." A co-founder gives you someone to hand the next meeting to after a bloodbath; a solo founder has to consciously leave the baggage at the door.

His workaround is building a village: an executive coach he can call anytime, a wife who has learned a lot about energy and software and is committed to the journey, and founder dinners. He almost skipped one on a Thursday because his week "had been such a train wreck" — and it turned out to be exactly what he needed, hearing other founders describe versions of the same experiences: "I'm not alone experiencing this thing on my own."

SL
Selina Li
gymii.ai · EP 2

Selina was a solo founder until December and is now "a big advocate of finding a co-founder." The pro of going solo: "you can really do whatever you want every day" with no one to report to.

Episode ↗
See Selina Li's full take

She names two things a co-founder gave her. First, a "check and balance" — regular check-ins to make sure they're working on their strengths and the highest-priority items for the company. Second, an antidote to isolation: she went from being on a sports team and then "an amazing team" in corporate to being "completely alone in the journey," where the day-to-day of what she was doing "was like kind of unknown to everyone." Bringing in a co-founder, and then a slightly expanded team, made it "a lot more fun too, because now we're all in it together." Her broader point: the to-do list of a startup is "endless" — engineering, business, legal, social media — so having someone take what they're best at moves the company "so much faster."
